# Contracts for work that is not a judgement

`verdict-contract.md` covers the case where something already exists and the
room is asked what it makes of it. A lot of useful persona work is not that.

Asking a persona to "endorse" a brainstorm produces nonsense: there is nothing
to endorse, "confidence: low" describes nothing, and the ideas end up filed
under a heading called *concerns*. Worse, a room that was never disagreeing gets
reported as suspicious unanimity, and — if the run is recorded — every seat
banks a false "endorse" against its track record.

So: three kinds of run, and the kind is recorded.

| `kind` | The room is asked to | Contract |
|---|---|---|
| `evaluative` (default) | judge something that exists | `verdict-contract.md` |
| `generative` | produce options from its lens | **contribution**, below |
| `reactions` | respond as itself, not assess | **reaction**, below |

Only `evaluative` runs feed persona track records. There is no such thing as
being right or wrong about an idea you proposed.

---

## The contribution contract

For `ideate`: brainstorms, angles, names, approaches, objections-to-anticipate.

```markdown
### Contributions
1. <the idea, stated concretely enough to act on>
   *why it's mine:* <the thing about your seat that produced it>
2. ...
3. ...

### The one I'd bet on
<which of yours, and what you're betting against>

### What I can't see here
<what your seat structurally misses about this question>
```

Rules:

- **Concrete or it does not count.** "Lean into trust" is not an idea. "Lead with
  'never get a surprise bill again' — it is the literal sentence in four Q2
  tickets" is.
- **`why it's mine` is the whole point.** If an idea could have come from any
  seat, it is filler. The value of a persona brainstorm is the idea only this
  lens produces.
- **No verdicts, no confidence.** You are not judging.
- **Quantity is not the goal.** Three ideas with real provenance beat twelve
  generic ones, and the synthesis will discard the generic ones anyway.

## The reaction contract

For `react`: market research, positioning tests, "how would this land?".

The persona is not an evaluator here. It is a person encountering the thing.

```markdown
### First reaction
<the unguarded response, in voice, before you have thought about it>

### What I do next
<the actual behaviour: ignore it, forward it, ask a question, look at a
competitor, buy. Be specific and be honest — "ignore it" is the most common
answer and the most useful one>

### What would make me care
<the change that moves you from that behaviour to a different one>

### What I'd say about it to a colleague
<one sentence, in your words, unpolished>
```

Rules:

- **Indifference is a finding.** Most people ignore most things. A room where
  every segment is intrigued is a room of flattering fictions.
- **Behaviour, not opinion.** "I'd find that interesting" is worthless. "I'd
  forward it to my finance lead and not follow up" is a result.
- **Stay in the world.** The persona does not know it is being consulted, does
  not comment on the strategy, and does not help. It reacts.

## Synthesizing a generative run

The chairman's job changes completely. It is not consensus versus dissent —
nobody was disagreeing.

1. **The set** — every idea, attributed, deduplicated.
2. **Clusters** — themes, and which seats converged on each. Convergence here is
   informative rather than suspicious: several lenses arriving independently at
   one theme is evidence, not groupthink.
3. **The unrepeatable ones** — ideas that only one seat's lens could have
   produced. This is the analogue of preserved dissent, and it is the entire
   return on running a room instead of asking once.
4. **What nobody proposed** — the blind-spot pass, unchanged and still the most
   valuable section.
5. **Recommended next** — what to test first and how, not what to adopt.

Never rank ideas by how many seats liked them. Nobody was voting, and the best
idea in a generative run is frequently the one only a single seat could see.

## Synthesizing a reactions run

1. **The split** — who cares, who does not, stated as behaviour.
2. **What landed and what did not** — with the actual words that landed.
3. **The objection nobody could answer** — if one recurs, it is the finding.
4. **What nobody mentioned** — including anything you expected them to.
5. **What to change**, and the cheapest way to test that it helped.

Do not average reactions into a sentiment. "Two would forward it, one would
ignore it, one would ask about price first" is usable. "Broadly positive" is not.
